Research

Safety for environmental perception

Automated vehicles must reliably perceive other road users and lane boundaries – even in adverse weather, at limited sensing range and under occlusion. My work addresses how to measure, improve and extend that perception safely.

Doctoral Thesis

Safety Enhancement for Environmental Perception in Automated Driving

Combining novel safety-oriented metrics, robust perception methods and new datasets to improve the safety of environmental perception – from criticality-aware evaluation of object and lane detection, through lane detection on snow-covered roads and collective lane detection, to safer multi-object tracking and infrastructure-assisted collective perception for vulnerable road users.

Faculty of Science, University of Tübingen Supervisor: Prof. Dr. Oliver Bringmann Second reviewer: Prof. Dr.-Ing. Cristóbal Curio

    Criticality Prediction

    Scenario-independent criticality assessment and prediction for vulnerable road users

    Safety evaluation hinges on knowing which objects actually matter. State-of-the-art criticality metrics are built for vehicles and specific scenarios. This work introduces a VRU-centric metric and machine-learning models that classify criticality across all scenarios, trained on a new labelled dataset derived from DeepAccident.

    0.94F1-score with Random Forest, vs. 0.36 for RSS
    +18 ppF1 gain of the VRU-centric metric
    161kframes from 691 accident and non-accident scenarios
    arXiv
    IEEE ITSC 2025 · Gold Coast, Australia

    SnowyLane

    Robust lane detection on snow-covered rural roads using infrastructural elements

    When snow covers the markings, camera-based lane detectors fail. SnowyLane introduces a CARLA dataset of a rural road in southern Germany and a method that detects roadside delineators in camera and LiDAR data, then models the lane layout with Bézier curves.

    80kframes across summer and winter conditions
    2D+3Dlane labels and delineator boxes
    5detectors evaluated, from YOLOv5 to PV-RCNN
    Paper (DOI) arXiv Project page
    IEEE VTC2025-Spring · Oslo, Norway

    CarlaNCAP

    Quantifying VRU safety in infrastructure-assisted collective perception using Euro NCAP scenarios

    Pedestrians and cyclists are most at risk in urban traffic. CarlaNCAP is a publicly available CARLA framework that recreates standardized Euro NCAP AEB VRU test scenarios at an intersection equipped with roadside units, and measures how much infrastructure sensors help an automated vehicle avoid collisions.

    100%accident prevention with ~4 well-placed RSUs
    12roadside camera/LiDAR positions evaluated
    33–100%prevention rate with vehicle sensors alone
    Paper (DOI) arXiv
    IEEE ITSC 2024 · Edmonton, Canada

    SCOPE

    A synthetic multimodal dataset for collective perception including physically correct weather conditions

    Vehicle-local perception is limited by occlusion and sensing range. SCOPE lets connected vehicles and roadside units share what they see, with a realistic LiDAR model, camera and LiDAR weather augmentation, vulnerable road users, and scenarios from motorways to digital twins of Karlsruhe and Tübingen.

    25collaborative agents (21 vehicles + 4 RSUs)
    60passive traffic participants per scene
    2digital twins of German towns
    Paper (DOI) arXiv
    IEEE VTC2024-Fall · Washington, D.C., USA

    LSM

    A comprehensive metric for assessing the safety of lane detection systems

    Precision and recall say little about whether a lane detector is safe. The Lane Safety Metric combines lateral detection accuracy, longitudinal detection range and scenario semantics such as road type, speed limits and VRUs into one comparable safety score.

    0–1single, comparable safety score
    3components: accuracy, range, scenario semantics
    Real + simapplicable to all lane types and weather
    Paper (DOI) arXiv
    IEEE ITSC 2020 · Virtual conference · Co-author

    Perception Safety Metric

    A comprehensive safety metric to evaluate perception in autonomous systems

    The starting point of my research: a single safety score for object detection and tracking that weighs detection quality, the relevance of each object via RSS safety distances and possible collision severity, and detection times within the safety-critical area. Evaluated on KITTI recordings and virtual VTD scenarios.

    1single, parametrizable safety score
    2D+3Dimage-plane and 3D detection & tracking
    RSSrelevance via safety distances
    Paper (DOI) arXiv
